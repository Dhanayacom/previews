What we know, and what we don't

We'd rather tell you less and have all of it be true.

What's been studied in dogs
  • Reishi. Forty healthy beagles ate it for four weeks. They accepted it well, and it didn't hurt digestion. One author worked for the ingredient maker. (Kayser et al., J Anim Sci, 2024)
  • A blend of Turkey Tail, Reishi, Lion's Mane and colostrum. Twenty healthy beagles, 12 weeks, with a placebo group. It was well tolerated, and gut bacteria shifted a little. It showed no immune benefit. A company that sells these products funded it. (Wang et al., J Anim Sci, 2026)
  • Lion's Mane. Eighteen older dogs ate large amounts for 16 weeks. Their gut bacteria changed. Memory and thinking weren't measured. (Cho et al., J Anim Sci Technol, 2022)
What hasn't been studied
  • Our chews. No study has tested any finished chew, ours included.
  • Lion's Mane for memory in dogs. We found no controlled trial.
  • Much of what you read online. It comes from studies in people, mice or cells in a lab. Dogs aren't small people.

What this means

These are small, short studies in healthy dogs, using specific extracts, often in larger amounts than a chew holds. They show these mushrooms were tolerated in those trials. They don't prove our chews do anything. When better evidence comes in, we'll add it here. Questions? Bring this page to your vet.
